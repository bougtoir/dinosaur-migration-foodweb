# JBI editorial fit audit (final hostile review)

1. Is the paper clearly biogeographic rather than merely methodological?
   Framed as a biogeographic inference problem throughout: the central
   question (when structural differences mimic or distort spatial
   turnover) is stated in the abstract Aim, Introduction paragraph 4, and
   the Discussion opening. PASS.

2. Is the explicit spatial/geographic component obvious by page 1?
   Yes — Introduction paragraph 1 leads with spatial differentiation,
   geographic turnover, connectivity, dispersal limitation and
   environmental filtering via beta diversity and distance decay. PASS.

3. Is distance decay sufficiently prominent?
   Distance decay appears in the title, abstract (Methods + Results),
   Introduction questions, Figure 3 (main figure), a dedicated Discussion
   section marked as central to biogeographic inference, and Table 1.
   PASS.

4. Does the manuscript test a clearly framed biogeographic inference
   problem? Yes — controlled generative simulations (Δβ_true = 0) plus
   factorial interaction map plus empirical deep-time stress test. PASS.

5. Are dinosaurs clearly an empirical stress test rather than a scope
   limitation? Yes — the Taxon abstract line states they are stress
   tests; the Introduction presents the fossil record as the extreme end
   of a general aggregation spectrum; no dinosaur-biology claims are
   made. PASS.

6. Does the paper advance basic understanding of how spatial
   biodiversity patterns are inferred? Yes — it quantifies magnitudes,
   joint interactions, decay-slope distortion and source-dependent
   temporal aggregation, and supplies a diagnostic workflow. PASS.

7. Is novelty stated as quantification + interaction + decay distortion
   + source-dependent aggregation rather than claiming each mechanism is
   new? Yes — Introduction explicitly states each mechanism is
   established and the gap is quantitative magnitude and joint effects.
   PASS.
