# JBI structured abstract (≤300 words)

**Aim** — To quantify when observed beta-diversity and distance-decay contrasts
represent genuine biogeographic spatial differentiation and when they are
generated, erased or exaggerated by structural properties of assemblage data.

**Location** — Simulated one- and two-dimensional landscapes; empirical case
studies from the Morrison Formation of western North America (Late Jurassic)
and the Nemegt Formation of Mongolia (Late Cretaceous).

**Taxon** — Terrestrial vertebrate assemblages, with herbivorous and predatory
dinosaur guilds used as empirical deep-time stress tests.

**Methods** — Controlled assemblage simulations in which the generative guild
contrast is zero by construction, manipulating regional pool size, dominance
structure, taxonomic aggregation (species→genus pooling), temporal aggregation
(eight slices → pooled bins) and sampling asymmetry, including a factorial
interaction design; a 2-D lattice replication with distance-decay slope
analysis tests geometric generality. Empirically, a sequential structural
decomposition of a strong predator–herbivore turnover contrast in the Morrison
Formation, plus an identical locked pipeline on Nemegt.

**Results** — Structural asymmetry alone generated false spatial
differentiation: a four-taxon pool produced bias ≈ −0.17 (extreme two-taxon
≈ −0.41; 2-D replication ≈ −0.21 / ≈ −0.47), and pool asymmetry also inflated
the apparent distance-decay slope (≈0.028 vs ≈0.006). Temporal aggregation
proved source-dependent: it erased the structurally generated component while
amplifying imposed ecological contrasts approximately four-fold. In Morrison, a
naive contrast of Δβ = −0.550 was progressively attenuated by structural
controls (Δβ = −0.020 after dominant-taxon exclusion); Nemegt showed no such
contrast (Δβ = +0.094, CI crossing zero).

**Main conclusions** — Beta-diversity and distance-decay contrasts should not
be interpreted as differences in spatial connectivity, dispersal or habitat
breadth without explicit control for assemblage structure and temporal
aggregation. Assemblage construction can create, erase or exaggerate apparent
geographic turnover, and it distorts the spatial form of turnover, not only
its mean.
