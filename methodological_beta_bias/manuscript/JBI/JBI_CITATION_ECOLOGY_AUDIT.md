# JBI citation ecology audit

Purpose: assess whether Journal of Biogeography papers can be integrated
where they directly support a claim — no citation gaming.

Already cited JBI papers: Nekola & White (1999, doi:10.1046/j.1365-2699.1999.00305.x)
— foundational distance-decay reference in Introduction and Discussion.

Candidate additions (all verified in Journal of Biogeography via Crossref):

1. Keil et al. (2012), "Patterns of beta diversity in Europe: the role of
   climate, land cover and distance across scales", JBI 39:1473–1486,
   doi:10.1111/j.1365-2699.2012.02701.x.
   Claim supported: beta diversity and distance decay are grain/extent
   scale-dependent. Location: Introduction, paragraph 1 (scale-dependence
   sentence). Decision: KEEP — integrated.

2. Sfenthourakis & Panitsa (2012), "From plots to islands: species
   diversity at different scales", JBI 39:750–759,
   doi:10.1111/j.1365-2699.2011.02639.x.
   Claim supported: diversity patterns depend on spatial scale; relevant
   to grain/extent caveat on turnover comparisons. Location: Introduction,
   paragraph 1 (same sentence). Decision: KEEP — integrated.

3. Stigall & Lieberman (2006), "Quantitative palaeobiogeography: GIS,
   phylogenetic biogeographical analysis, and conservation insights",
   JBI 33:2051–2060, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2699.2006.01585.x.
   Claim supported: deep-time assemblages as a domain of formal
   biogeographic inference, not a special case. Location: Introduction,
   paragraph 3 (fossil-record-as-stress-test sentence). Decision: KEEP —
   integrated.

No further JBI papers were added: additional candidates (e.g. further
distance-decay or species-pool papers) would duplicate claims already
covered by Nekola & White (1999), Keil et al. (2012) and the existing
non-JBI literature; adding them would be citation stacking rather than
supporting a specific claim.

Result: 3 JBI references added (total JBI citations in manuscript: 4).
All DOIs verified via Crossref.
