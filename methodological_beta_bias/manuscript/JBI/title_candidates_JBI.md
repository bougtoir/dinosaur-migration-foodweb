# Final title shortlist — JBI (6 candidates)

1. **Assemblage structure distorts beta diversity and distance decay** —
   RECOMMENDED (69 chars). Neutral direction ('distorts' covers
   create/erase/exaggerate), foregrounds the two core biogeographic tools,
   no taxon or formation names, matches the cover letter.
2. Structural asymmetries bias biogeographic inference from beta diversity —
   inference-first framing; accurate but 'bias' as verb is slightly narrower.
3. Assemblage structure reshapes spatial turnover and distance decay —
   'reshapes' is vivid; slightly weaker on the false-differentiation result.
4. When beta diversity misrepresents biogeographic differentiation —
   readable, conceptual; interrogative and less specific.
5. Structural asymmetries distort spatial turnover among guilds —
   guilds foregrounded; drops distance decay.
6. Regional diversity and temporal aggregation bias biogeographic turnover —
   names two mechanisms only; accurate but partial.

Selection rationale: candidate 1 is ≤115 characters, contains no
abbreviations, avoids dinosaur/Allosaurus/Morrison terms, and puts both
beta diversity and distance decay up front — the two instruments JBI
readers use for spatial-turnover inference. 'Distorts' was chosen over
'create, erase and exaggerate' because the temporal-aggregation direction
is source-dependent.

Running title (≤40 chars): **Bias in spatial turnover inference** (34 chars)
