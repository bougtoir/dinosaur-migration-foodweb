## Materials and Methods

### Overview

The study combines controlled simulations of guild assemblages, in which the generative turnover contrast is known, with a sequential empirical decomposition of a real guild-level contrast in the Morrison Formation and a structural comparator from the Nemegt Formation. All estimators, null models and random seeds are fixed in the analysis code; every number reported in this article is traced to its generating script and output file in a value-level registry (see Data Accessibility Statement).

### Generative process, observed estimand and structural manipulation

Three elements are kept distinct throughout. The generative process is the simulation rule that places taxa in space; in the simulations both guilds are generated by the same per-taxon process unless a scenario explicitly imposes an ecological difference. The observed estimand is the guild contrast in mean pairwise dissimilarity computed from the resulting incidence matrices. A structural manipulation changes how the assemblage data are constructed (pool size, dominance, taxonomic resolution, temporal pooling or sampling) without changing the generative process. Beta diversity was measured as the mean pairwise Jaccard dissimilarity among sampling units, computed on incidence matrices (Legendre, 2014). The focal contrast is

[[EQ:delta]]

where β_P and β_H are the mean pairwise Jaccard dissimilarities of the predator and herbivore guilds. The same estimand is used for point estimates and bootstrap distributions. In each simulated scenario, the simulation-defined (generative) contrast Δβ_true is the contrast expected under the generative process alone; it equals zero by construction in every scenario except the imposed-contrast arm of the temporal-aggregation simulation. Bias is defined as

[[EQ:bias]]

Negative Δβ indicates that predators are more spatially homogeneous than herbivores. For taxonomic lumping, the effect on the manipulated guild is reported as

[[EQ:lumping]]

so that positive values mean that genus pooling makes the guild appear more spatially continuous; its effect on Δβ is reported separately with explicit sign. The conceptual decomposition

[[EQ:decomp]]

is used only as a scaffold for naming the structural terms (pool size, dominance, temporal aggregation, lumping and sampling), not as a fitted causal partition; because the terms can interact, interaction was examined with a factorial design (Simulation 6).

### Simulation design

Assemblages were generated on a one-dimensional axis of 200 localities (`analysis/sim_core.py`). Each taxon occupies a contiguous range whose width is a fixed fraction of the axis (default 0.45), centred at a uniform random location, and is recorded with probability 0.35 at each locality within its range. Herbivore (26 taxa by default) and predator (12 taxa by default) incidence matrices are built independently from identical per-taxon parameters, so the two guilds share the same spatial process and differ only in the manipulated structural property. The simulator is deliberately simple: its outputs are demonstrations of sufficiency under specified conditions, not calibrated estimates for real landscapes. Each parameter cell was replicated 100–200 times with fixed seeds (seed set 20260922 for Simulations 1–5 and 20260923 for Simulation 6); the complete parameter and seed list is provided in the repository.

Simulation 1 (pool size) held herbivore richness at 26 taxa and varied predator richness from 2 to 26 (200 replicates per cell); Δβ_true = 0. Outputs were the mean Δβ_obs, its 95% replicate interval, the proportion of replicates with negative Δβ_obs, and the proportion exceeding an operational false-contrast threshold of 0.1 in absolute value. Sensitivity of the false-contrast rate to thresholds of 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 was evaluated at pools of 2, 4, 12 and 26 taxa (200 replicates per cell). Simulation 2 (dominance) elevated one predator taxon to occur in a proportion p_D of all localities, independently of its range, with p_D varied from 0 to 0.9. Simulation 3 (taxonomic lumping) collapsed k geographically partitioned species (disjoint sub-ranges) into a single genus column, with k from 1 to 6. Simulation 4 (temporal aggregation, structural arm) redrew each taxon's occurrences independently in eight time slices and pooled locality rows into 8, 4, 2 or 1 temporal bins, with pooled presence defined as presence in any pooled slice (union pooling); because the guilds share per-taxon parameters, the only nonzero contrast at the finest resolution is the structural pool-size term. Simulation 5 (sampling asymmetry) divided the axis into alternating blocks of 20 localities and retained predator occurrences in one block type with a sampling multiplier of 1–8 relative to the other. Simulation 6 (factorial) crossed predator pool size (4, 12 or 26 taxa), dominant-taxon prevalence (p_D = 0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 or 0.9) and lumping (k = 1, 3 or 6 species pooled into one column), with 100 replicates per cell. When k equals or exceeds the pool size, the entire small guild is collapsed into a single column; such cells were retained but are identified as degenerate when reported.

### Temporal aggregation under imposed ecological contrasts

To test how temporal aggregation acts on an explicitly ecological contrast, Simulation 4b gave the two guilds different mean range breadths, a difference in spatial ecology rather than in data construction (narrower ranges yield higher within-guild turnover). Three prospectively specified scenarios were run with herbivore and predator breadths of 0.30 and 0.45 (weak), 0.30 and 0.70 (moderate), and 0.15 and 0.90 (strong). Pool sizes followed the structural arm (26 and 12 taxa) and aggregation used the same union-pooling rule (150 replicates; seed 20260925; `analysis/21_temporal_false_negative.py`). Because the pool asymmetry is retained, each finest-resolution contrast combines the imposed ecological difference with the structural component quantified in Simulation 4. The change under pooling was summarised as the attenuation fraction

[[EQ:atten]]

computed on replicate means, where negative values indicate amplification rather than attenuation.

### Two-dimensional replication and distance decay

A confirmatory simulator generated assemblages on a 40 × 40 lattice; each taxon occupied a disc of radius 0.225 times the lattice width centred at a uniform random location and was recorded with probability 0.35 per occupied cell (`analysis/13_2d_robustness.py`; 100 replicates; seed 20260924). In addition to mean pairwise dissimilarity, I computed distance-decay slopes as the ordinary least-squares slope of pairwise Jaccard dissimilarity on pairwise Euclidean distance (lattice units) for each replicate and guild, so that the apparent decay of a small-pool guild could be compared with that of the comparison guild under identical generative spatial processes. The same lattice was used for a structural-only temporal-aggregation check (8, 4 and 1 bins).

### Empirical worked example: Morrison Formation

Occurrence data were taken from the Morrison Formation dataset of Maidment (2024), archived in Dryad under a CC0 waiver; Morrison dinosaur assemblages have a long history of palaeoecological interpretation (Noto & Grossman, 2010). The cleaned dinosaur subset comprises [[emp_n_occurrences|int]] occurrences in [[emp_n_collections|int]] collections and [[emp_n_genera|int]] resolved genera ([[emp_n_genera_herbivore|int]] herbivore and [[emp_n_genera_predator|int]] predator genera). Guilds follow the published taxonomy: predators are theropods; herbivores are ornithischians and sauropodomorphs. Analyses used the collection as the sampling unit, genus resolution, the Jaccard metric and a 999-replicate collection bootstrap (`analysis/07_morrison_empirical_decomposition.py`; seed 20260924). The sequential decomposition applied, in fixed order: (1) the naive genus-level Δβ; (2) a gamma-matched null (10,000 replicates drawing herbivore genera to match the predator pool size); (3) a frequency-matched null (10,000 replicates matching the occupancy frequencies of predator genera); (4) exclusion of the dominant predator, *Allosaurus*; (5) species-level resolution, with *Allosaurus* split into its named species; (6) a regression of geographic extent on stratigraphic duration and occurrence count across genera, reporting the *Allosaurus* residual and z-score; and (7) an exploratory logistic model of *Allosaurus* presence on collection covariates (environment, lithology, member, state, systems tract and dinosaur richness), reported only as a pseudo-R². The unpenalised fit showed quasi-separation and did not fully converge, so an L2-penalised refit (regularisation strengths C = 0.1, 1 and 10) is reported alongside it; both are exploratory and carry no inferential weight.

### Structural comparator: Nemegt Formation

Nemegt Formation dinosaur occurrences (Maastrichtian, Mongolia) were downloaded from the Paleobiology Database through its public API (Peters & McClennen, 2016), with download parameters and SHA-256 checksums recorded: [[nem_n_occurrences|int]] dinosaur occurrences in [[nem_n_collections|int]] collections. The body-fossil, genus-resolved subset used for analysis contained [[nem_sub_n_collections|int]] collections, with [[nem_sub_n_occ_predator|int]] predator occurrences of [[nem_sub_n_genera_predator|int]] genera and [[nem_sub_n_occ_herbivore|int]] herbivore occurrences of [[nem_sub_n_genera_herbivore|int]] genera. The same locked specification (Jaccard, collection level, genus resolution, 999-replicate bootstrap; seed 20260920; `analysis/11_nemegt_validation.py`) yielded the comparator Δβ. Because the Nemegt herbivore pool is smaller than the predator pool, the gamma-matched null is undefined there, and Nemegt is used strictly as a comparator of reversed structure, not as a biological replication.

### Independent evidence audit

A bounded literature audit classified independent evidence relevant to predator mobility in the Morrison Formation (isotopes, trackways, feeding traces, ontogenetic structure and palaeobiogeography) on a five-level scale from support for exceptional mobility to contradiction. The full classification is provided in the repository.

### Statistical summaries and reproducibility

Simulation intervals are 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles across replicates; empirical intervals are bootstrap percentile intervals; null intervals are 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of the null distributions and are reported separately from bootstrap intervals. No significance tests are used to make claims about the simulations, whose purpose is to quantify magnitudes. All analyses were run in Python (NumPy, pandas, SciPy, statsmodels and scikit-learn). The scripts under `methodological_beta_bias/analysis/` regenerate every table, figure and registry value from the archived inputs.
