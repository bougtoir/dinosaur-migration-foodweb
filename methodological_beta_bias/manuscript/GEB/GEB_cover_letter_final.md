# Cover letter — Global Ecology and Biogeography

Dear Editors,

We submit our manuscript "Structural asymmetries distort guild-level
beta-diversity contrasts" for consideration as a Research Article in
Global Ecology and Biogeography.

Beta diversity and distance decay are core instruments of
macroecology and biogeography, yet comparative studies routinely
contrast assemblages whose construction differs — in regional pool
size, dominance, taxonomic grain, temporal integration and sampling
effort. Using controlled simulations in which the generative guild
contrast is known, we quantify how large purely structural contrasts
can become, on one- and two-dimensional spatial layouts, and we show
that the same asymmetries distort not only mean turnover but the
slope of distance-decay relationships themselves. Temporal
aggregation proves source-dependent rather than simply attenuating:
it erased structurally generated contrasts while amplifying
ecological contrasts generated by range breadths approximately
four-fold. An empirical stress test on Late Jurassic dinosaur
assemblages demonstrates the diagnostic cascade in practice, and the
same logic conceptually extends to museum collections, herbarium
records, pooled monitoring data and other aggregated occurrence
compilations. We close with a practical diagnostic workflow intended
to precede ecological interpretation of guild-level turnover
contrasts.

The manuscript contains no identifying information in the blinded
main text; identified materials (title page, acknowledgements) are
provided separately. All data derive from public sources and all
code, frozen seeds and value-level traceability are documented in
the data and code availability statement.

Tatsuki Onishi
